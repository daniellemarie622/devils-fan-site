Historical Moments & Team Legacy

The New Jersey Devils built their identity through disciplined hockey, standout goaltending and three Stanley Cup championships. Their story began before New Jersey, however, with two earlier franchises: the Kansas City Scouts and the Colorado Rockies.

From Kansas City to New Jersey

1974–1976 • Kansas City Scouts

The franchise entered the NHL as the Kansas City Scouts in 1974. After two seasons, the club relocated to Denver.

1976–1982 • Colorado Rockies

In Colorado, the team became the Rockies. Financial and competitive struggles continued, and the franchise was sold and moved again in 1982.

1982–present • New Jersey Devils

The team settled in New Jersey, adopted the Devils name and began playing at the Brendan Byrne Arena in East Rutherford. The name was selected through a fan poll and references the legendary Jersey Devil associated with the Pine Barrens.

The “Mickey Mouse” Comment

On November 19, 1983, after the Devils lost 13–4 to the Edmonton Oilers, Wayne Gretzky criticized the club as a “Mickey Mouse operation.” Devils fans later embraced the remark, wearing Mickey Mouse gear when Edmonton returned to New Jersey. The episode became a memorable part of the team's early identity.

The Golden Era & the Neutral Zone Trap (1993–2003)

Under head coach Jacques Lemaire, the Devils became known for a structured defensive system often called the Neutral Zone Trap. The approach clogged the middle of the ice, disrupted opponents' transitions and created counterattack opportunities.

With leaders such as Scott Stevens, Scott Niedermayer and goaltender Martin Brodeur, New Jersey became one of the NHL's dominant teams of the era and won the Stanley Cup in 1995, 2000 and 2003.
